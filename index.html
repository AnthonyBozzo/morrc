MORRC: McGill Ortho Resident Research Curriculum

Eighteen self-paced, interactive modules that take you from framing your first clinical question as an R1 to presenting your own project at Research Day as an R5. Every example is orthopaedic, and every quiz teaches as well as tests.

Why MORRC?

Our goal is to make every McGill orthopaedic resident a confident consumer of evidence and a competent producer of it.

By the end of residency you should be able to…

  • frame a focused, answerable clinical question;
  • choose and defend a study design;
  • critically appraise any orthopaedic paper for bias and clinical relevance;
  • take a project from protocol to publication with integrity.

How it is built

  • Real orthopaedic papers, not abstractions: appraisal is learned by doing.
  • Retrieval practice: every quiz answer comes with an explanation.
  • Spiral design: bias, precision and clinical importance return with more depth each year.
  • Linked to your work: journal club, Research Day and your own project. Maps to the CanMEDS Scholar role and CBD.
How modules work Each module takes about 60–90 minutes: a 20–30 minute core lesson, an interactive case, and a 12-item quiz. Get at least 10 of 12 correct (83%) to pass the quiz and complete the module; attempts are unlimited and question order is shuffled each time. Nothing is locked, so you can work out of sequence. Senior residents can test out of a module by passing its quiz on the first attempt.

Three tiers, R1 to R5

About 8–10 hours of module time per year, paced around journal club and Research Day.
